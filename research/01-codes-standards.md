# 01 — Codes and standards for the ground–façade edge (Vancouver, BC)

Researched: 2026-10-12. Layer: CODES AND STANDARDS (regulatory baseline).

## 1. Summary

- **The governing code in Vancouver is the 2025 Vancouver Building By-law (VBBL 2025, By-law #14343).** The BC Building Code does not apply in the City of Vancouver. VBBL 2025 came into effect on 15 September 2025, replaced VBBL 2019, and is based on BCBC 2024, which is in turn based on the NBC.
- **Part 9 of Division B covers most buildings the tool will see.** It applies to buildings of up to 3 storeys and up to 600 m² building area in residential, business, mercantile or low/medium-hazard industrial occupancies. Larger buildings use Part 5 (Environmental Separation), and so do cladding/substrate combinations that Part 9 does not list.
- **A rainscreen with a capillary break is mandatory in practice.**
  - The By-law requires two planes of protection on walls that enclose residential occupancy (9.27.2.2.(4)).
  - It also requires a capillary break where heating degree-days are below 3400 and the moisture index is above 0.90 (9.27.2.2.(5)).
  - Vancouver's own climate table gives HDD 2825–2925 and MI 1.4, so the trigger is always met.
  - A Part 5 explanatory note states that rainscreen is the minimum acceptable option for vertical walls in Part 5 buildings.
- **Key numeric limits at the base of the wall:**
  - Moisture-sensitive cladding must sit at least 200 mm above finished ground (9.27.2.4).
  - Stucco must sit at least 200 mm above finished ground, except over concrete or masonry (9.28.1.4).
  - Foundation walls must extend at least 150 mm above finished ground (9.15.4.6).
  - Structural wood less than 150 mm above ground must be preservative-treated (9.3.2.9), to the use categories of CSA O80.1.
  - Untreated framing on concrete that is in contact with the ground needs a separator of at least 0.05 mm polyethylene or Type S roll roofing, unless the wood is at least 150 mm above ground (9.23.2.3).
- **Flashing:**
  - It is required at every horizontal cladding junction, offset or substrate change, and above openings when the eave is too high relative to its overhang (9.27.3.8).
  - Each flashing needs a 50 mm upstand, a slope of at least 6%, 25 mm end dams in Vancouver, a lap of at least 10 mm and a drip offset of at least 5 mm.
- **Grading, drainage and damp-proofing:**
  - Surface grading is performance-only: water must not accumulate at or near the building. The code gives no slope value.
  - Foundation drainage and damp-proofing (9.13, 9.14) are prescriptive below grade.
- **Exterior seating:** no code governs a façade bench as such. A guard rule applies only where a walking surface is more than 600 mm above the adjacent surface.

## 3. Unverified

- **U1. BCBC 2024 and NBC 2020 text.** I could not read the BCBC 2024 PDF: it is 16 MB and over the fetch limit, and no free HTML version exists on bcpublications. VBBL 2025 is stated to align with BCBC 2024, but I did not compare clause by clause. To confirm, read BCBC 2024 Div. B 9.27.2.2, 9.27.2.4, 9.27.3.8 and 9.3.2.9 from the gov.bc.ca PDF.
- **U2. Which provisions are Vancouver-only.** Two provisions look Vancouver-specific: VBBL 9.27.2.2.(4) (two planes for residential walls) and 5.6.1.2.(3) (flashings, drips and overhangs). This needs a side-by-side check with NBC 2020 / BCBC 2024. It matters only if the tool later covers other BC municipalities.
- **U3. How "finished ground" is defined and measured.** The search found BC Building Code Appeal Board decision #1114, "Finished Ground Level", at https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/construction-industry/building-codes-standards/building-code-appeal-board/building-code-appeal-board-decisions/bcab-1114, but I did not read it. Also needed: whether paving, decking or gravel strips count as finished ground for the 200 mm and 150 mm rules.
- **U4. Clearances for non-moisture-sensitive or treated cladding.** 9.27.2.4 applies only to cladding "adversely affected by moisture". Whether treated wood, fibre-cement, metal or masonry may come closer than 200 mm is not stated. Manufacturer instructions (for example fibre-cement) probably set their own minimums; not researched.
- **U5. Termite clauses.** I could not find any designation of Vancouver as a locality "where termites are known to occur". The tool currently assumes 9.3.2.9.(1)–(2) do not apply; this needs confirmation.
- **U6. Wording of 9.27.5.3 (furring), 9.27.5.5/9.27.5.7 (fasteners) and 9.14.3–9.14.5 values in the 2025 edition.** These articles exist in 2025 but their exact text was read only in VBBL 2019. Confirm in VBBL25-V2.
- **U7. CSA A23.1 exposure classes** (e.g. C-1, C-2, F-1, F-2) for a reinforced concrete plinth or seat in the splash zone, and the full CSA O80 Use Category tables. Both standards are paywalled and were not read. Part 9 avoids exposure classes for unreinforced concrete.
- **U8. 9.23.2.4 "Connections to Preservative-Treated Wood".** I saw the heading in VBBL 2025 but did not read the text. It probably governs fastener and connector compatibility with treated sole plates and seat framing.
- **U9. Book II (Plumbing) Art. 2.4.2.5 and 2.4.10.4,** referenced for foundation drain and roof/balcony drainage connections. Not read.

## 4. Gaps (likely needed by the tool, not found in code)

- **No numeric slope for grade away from the building.** VBBL 9.14.6.1 and 5.7.1.2 are performance-only. A fall value (for example x% over y m) must come from guides such as BC Housing or CMHC (not yet sourced) and be labelled as guidance.
- **No exposure or splash-zone classification.** The code has no rain exposure categories by orientation, height or overhang ratio, and no splash height beyond the 200 mm cladding clearance. BC Housing / HPO building envelope guides are probable secondary sources.
- **No minimum overhang.** The only overhang-based rule is the eave-height to overhang ratio for flashing over openings (9.27.3.8.(3)). Part 5 Note A-5.6.1.2.(3) is qualitative.
- **No requirement on drainage strips or gravel beds at the wall base,** and none on planting or soil against the wall beyond the clearances.
- **Hard landscaping against the wall.** Clearance rules where paving, decks or patios meet the wall (and how to measure from them) are not covered; see U3.
- **No by-law requirement for the DPC/separation between a concrete plinth and the wall assembly beyond 9.23.2.3.** That clause asks only for 0.05 mm polyethylene or Type S roll roofing, and only below 150 mm. Detailing practice (for example a sill gasket plus membrane) needs guide evidence.
- **Thermal and energy rules at the plinth** (VBBL Part 10 / 9.36 insulation continuity, below-grade wall RSI) were not researched.
- **Zoning and City rainwater management.** Projection of overhangs and seats into setbacks, and on-site rainwater rules, were not researched. They likely constrain overhang depth and downspout discharge.
- **Lime, clay and natural renders and other non-listed claddings** fall to Part 5 under 9.27.1.1.(6). There is no prescriptive baseline for them; they need professional-design flags.
- **Fire separation (spatial separation, 9.10.14/9.10.15)** for combustible cladding near property lines was not researched. It may limit timber cladding choices on narrow lots.
