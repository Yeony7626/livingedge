# 03 — Assembly performance and climate for the ground–façade edge (Vancouver, BC)

Researched: 2026-10-12. Layers: ASSEMBLY PERFORMANCE (how the layers work together) and CLIMATE (Vancouver loads).

Scope note: code clauses below were read from the **VBBL 2014** text on free.bcpublications.ca. That was the edition reachable as HTML. `01-codes-standards.md` covers the current VBBL 2025 and should be treated as the authoritative edition. The 2014 clauses quoted here match the 2025 values reported in 01 where both exist: 200 mm cladding clearance, 200 mm stucco clearance, the 150 mm wood/concrete separation rule, flashing with a 5 mm drip and 6% slope, and the capillary-break trigger. They are used here for **assembly logic**, not as the code baseline.

## 1. Short summary

- **Overhangs work, and the effect is measurable in Vancouver, but small samples dominate.**
  - In RDH field monitoring of Lower Mainland buildings, wind-driven rain was measured 4 m below the roofline:
    - About 3% of horizontal rainfall on an east wall under a 500 mm overhang.
    - About 10% under a 50 mm overhang.
    - About 13% with no overhang at 3 m below the roof.
    - That is roughly a one-third to one-quarter reduction factor.
  - BSC (Straube) gives about 50% reduction in deposition from overhangs plus peaked roofs.
  - The 1996 CMHC/Morrison Hershfield survey of 46 coastal BC buildings found failure rates falling as overhang depth rose. The often-quoted figures are about 90% of walls with problems at no overhang versus 25% at about 600 mm. I could verify these only via a secondary source.
  - The code's own criterion is strict. A wall counts as "sheltered" (no head flashing over openings) only when it lies within ¼ of the overhang depth below the eave. A ground-level wall base on a normal building is therefore never fully protected by an overhang.
- **Wind-driven rain is semi-empirically predictable.**
  - Wall rain ≈ DRF × wind speed × rain rate × cos(angle) × RDF × exposure factors, with DRF ≈ 0.2–0.25 (Straube, BSD-148).
  - These simple models can be off by a factor of 2–5 at individual façade points, so use them for relative effects only.
  - RDH found **east** to be the primary wind-driven-rain direction in Vancouver: east gauges caught 3× the south gauges. ECCC's most frequent wind direction at YVR is also east (all weather).
  - A BC Housing maintenance bulletin instead says storms in south-west BC come "from the south and sides". This conflict is unresolved.
  - Wetting falls toward the base of taller façades. On one six-storey building, the 3rd floor got 38% of the 6th floor's driving rain.
- **Wall-base protections that are evidenced:**
  - **Clearances:** cladding at least 200 mm above grade, stucco at least 200 mm, and wood at least 150 mm above ground or else separated from concrete.
  - **Splash zone:** German practice defines it as 0–300 mm above grade (manufacturer guide).
  - **Capillary break:** at least 9.5 mm, required in Vancouver because MI 1.44 > 0.90 and HDD < 3400.
  - **Drips:** at least 5 mm offset. Lab tests show unhemmed kick-out drips shed over 90% of sill water within 1.2 m.
  - **Foundation drain:** 150 mm clean stone, with no more than 10% passing a 4 mm sieve.
  - **Ventilated cavity:** 19 mm dries better than 10 mm, with diminishing returns beyond that.
- **Drying:**
  - North-facing and tree-shaded walls in West Vancouver stayed wetter and dried more slowly than south and east walls. Poor detailing outweighed orientation.
  - Sheathing in monitored Vancouver rainscreen walls was dry May–September and re-wetted from October.
- **Ageing and maintenance:**
  - BC Housing bulletins give review and cleaning cycles: annual review, cleaning every few years with non-pressurised water, and repainting of wood at about 7+ years.
  - Weathering erosion of bare wood surfaces is about 34–101 µm per year, which is roughly 1–3 mm over 30 years.
  - Ground-contact decay data, drainage-gravel silting rates and seat abrasion from use were **not** verified. These are the main gaps.
- **Climate (ECCC, Vancouver Int'l A, 1981–2010 normals, read directly from the ECCC API):**
  - About 1,153 mm of rain a year, on 165 rain days.
  - Days per year by daily rain total: 75 days at or above 5 mm, 41 at or above 10 mm, and 6.6 at or above 25 mm.
  - November is the wettest month (186 mm); July is the driest (36 mm).
  - Mean RH is 86% at 06:00 and 70% at 15:00.
  - About 41 days a year have a minimum at or below 0 °C, which gives about 37 days a year with an air-temperature freeze–thaw cycle (derived).
  - Bright sunshine is 1,938 h a year: 56 h in December and 290 h in July.
- **Code design climate (VBBL 2014, City of Vancouver):**
  - 15-minute rain: 10 mm.
  - One-day rain (1/50): 112 mm.
  - Annual rain: 1,325 mm.
  - Moisture index: 1.44.
  - Driving-rain wind pressure (1/5): 160 Pa.

## 3. Proposed construction sequence for the typical edge (for assembly animation)

The order follows Part 9 dependencies: each step must exist before the next can be built or inspected. Code clauses are VBBL 2014 (see 01 for VBBL 2025). Steps marked † come from general practice and were **not** confirmed by a source read here.

| # | Step (layer appears) | Why it comes here / what it must satisfy | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Excavate; place granular drainage layer or prepare bearing soil; pour **footing** | The drainage layer, where used, is at least 125 mm under the footings and extends 300 mm beyond them. | VBBL 2014 9.14.4.2 |
| 2 | Build **foundation wall / plinth** (cast concrete or masonry); seal form-tie holes; parge masonry (≥6 mm, coved over footing) | The surface must be prepared before damp-proofing. | VBBL 2014 9.13.2.4 |
| 3 | Apply **damp-proofing** to the exterior below-grade face | Applied over parging or concrete below ground level, before backfill. | VBBL 2014 9.13.2.1, 9.13.2.5 |
| 4 | Lay **foundation drain tile** at the footing; cover with **≥150 mm clean stone** (≤10% passing 4 mm); connect to the disposal point | Must be in place before backfill. | VBBL 2014 9.14.3.3, 9.14.5.1 |
| 5 | **Backfill**, kept clean within 600 mm and placed without damaging the damp-proofing or drain | Graded so that after settling it does not drain toward the wall. † Backfill is often delayed until the floor structure braces the wall. | VBBL 2014 9.12.3.1–9.12.3.3 |
| 6 | **Sill separation (DPC / sill gasket)** or a preservative-treated sill plate on the foundation top | Required for untreated wood on concrete in ground contact unless the wood is ≥150 mm above ground. This is the edge's capillary break between plinth and timber. | VBBL 2014 9.23.2.3 |
| 7 | **Wall framing + sheathing** | Substrate for the membrane and flashing. | † (general practice) |
| 8 | **Roof and overhang** framed and dried in | † Usually before cladding so that the wall is protected during construction; this order is not stated in the sources read. The overhang then shelters the wall below (ev.assembly.overhang-*). | † |
| 9 | **Base-of-wall flashing** at the plinth/cladding junction (slope ≥6%, drip ≥5 mm proud of the face below, laps ≥10 mm, end dams) | Must exist before the sheathing membrane so that the membrane can lap over its upstand. | VBBL 2014 9.27.3.8.(4) |
| 10 | **Sheathing membrane / WRB** shingle-lapped over the flashing upstand (≥50 mm) | Water on the membrane is directed out over the flashing. | VBBL 2014 9.27.3.8.(4)(a) |
| 11 | **Insect screen at the cavity base + vertical strapping** (cavity ≥9.5 mm; 19 mm preferred for drying) | Creates the capillary break and drained, vented cavity. | VBBL 2014 9.27.2.2; Ge & Ye 2007; BSD-013 |
| 12 | **Cladding / render**, with its bottom edge ≥200 mm above the *final* finished grade | Clearance protects against splash and pooling. The final grade level must be fixed before this step (Caparol stresses agreeing it across trades). | VBBL 2014 9.27.2.4, 9.28.1.4; Caparol *Sockel* |
| 13 | **Final grading** sloped away; **gravel strip** or paving at the wall base | Water must not accumulate at the building. Gravel versus paving has only illustrative evidence. | VBBL 2014 9.14.6.1, 9.12.3.2; ev.assembly.splash-gravel-vs-hard |
| 14 | **Landscape / soil** beyond the strip; downspout discharge | Downspouts must conform to 9.26.18.2 (not read). | VBBL 2014 9.14.6.5 |
| 15 | **Seat** (timber bench) installed last, kept clear of the cladding and the splash zone † | No code governs a façade bench (see 01). Placing it last avoids damage during construction. | † |

## 4. Unverified and gaps

### Unverified (seen only in search-engine summaries or secondary sources; not read in the primary)

- **1996 survey overhang percentages** ("nearly 90% at 0 mm, 25% at ~600 mm"). The bands 0, 1–300, 301–600 and >600 mm are reported as appearing in a 2023 BCBEC/RDH presentation chart (https://bcbec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/1-DR-Worst-to-Best.pdf). The original PDF (https://www.aivc.org/sites/default/files/airbase_11608.pdf, 68 pp.) has no machine-readable text layer.
- **Structure magazine estimate:** a 2 ft overhang may reduce the likelihood of moisture intrusion by over 50% (https://structuremag.org/wp-content/uploads/C-GuestColumn-Lockyear-Apr121.pdf). Not read.
- **Finch, Wang & Wong:** *Using Overhangs to Reduce Building Facade Exposure to Rain* (RDH / FPInnovations). The quantified field results were not obtained; only the landing page was read. The report link points to bchousing.org. This is likely the best quantitative overhang source for Vancouver mid-rises.
- **CWC / FPInnovations ground-contact stake tests** (Maple Ridge BC and Petawawa ON, installed 2004–05):
  - Reported in summaries: moderate-to-high decay after 5 years even for western redcedar and yellow cypress heartwood; untreated durable heartwood not recommended for long-term ground contact.
  - Page returned 403: https://cwc.ca/why-build-with-wood/durability/durability-research-and-development
- **BRE 10-year untreated post trial:** most untreated posts lasted under 5 years (https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/?p=152945). Not read.
- **Wang & Morris (2008):** recalculated Scheffer climate index and decay hazard map for North America (https://woodpreservation.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/wang29.pdf). A Vancouver value was not found. The Scheffer formula was not verified, so I did not compute an index from the ECCC data.
- **BC Housing Maintenance Matters No. 4:** sealants may need replacing about every 10 years. Seen in a search summary only.
- **BC Housing renewals guide:** roofing membranes typically replaced every 20–25 years. Seen in a search summary only.
- **UK Approved Document C:** DPC at least 150 mm above adjoining ground to resist splash-back. Quoted only via forums and trade sites; the gov.uk FAQ PDF text was not extractable.
- **German WTA Merkblatt 4-9:** plinth must be splash-proof to 30 cm above ground. Seen only via a renovation handbook in a search summary. The Caparol zoning in 2a was read directly.
- **Tariku & Ge (BCIT exposure facility):** rainscreen-wall plywood dried faster in spring and wetted faster in autumn than plywood in conventional walls. The repository returned 503.
- **NRC intent statement for 9.27.2.4:** limits splash, soak and pool against lower cladding (https://codes-guides.nrc.ca/IA/05NBC/nbc05ei005443.html). Search summary only.
- **Hygrothermal-model orientation caveat** (hygIRC vs WUFI, Vancouver): see ev.assembly.orientation-model-caveat. Search summary only.
- **Stucco cracking:** drivers are shrinkage, thermal movement, sheathing gaps, poor moist-curing and crack concentration at opening corners, with panels of no more than 144 sq ft (≈13 m²) between control joints per PCA/ASTM-type guidance. Seen only in trade-press summaries (Sika technical bulletin; Walls & Ceilings). No coastal BC stucco ageing study was found.
- **Wood erosion vs exposure angle** (Williams et al., Part III): the direction of the angle effect could not be confirmed from the summary.
- **Comparison studies of wind-driven-rain models:** ISO and Straube–Burnett errors of 2–5× versus CFD at about 20–25%. Search summary of Heriot-Watt / TU Eindhoven records.

### Gaps (no usable evidence found in this pass)

1. **Splash-back height measurements** at a wall base versus ground surface (soil, gravel, paving) and drip-line distance. Only code minimums (200 mm), a German zoning convention (300 mm) and anecdotes were found. Gravel-versus-paving coefficients must stay **illustrative**.
2. **Overhang effect at the wall base of low-rise buildings.** All Vancouver field data are 3–6 storey, gauges are placed high, and no splash is included. The ratio rule in 2a is extrapolated.
3. **Silting or clogging rates of drainage gravel and gravel strips.** No residential field data were found. Highway geocomposite drains performed for at least 5 years in soils with up to 43% fines (TRB record, not read). Rules must be illustrative.
4. **Service lives of edge components:** sealants, base flashings, DPC membranes, renders, gravel-strip renewal and treated timber in Vancouver. BC Housing bulletins give maintenance intervals but few lives. CSA S478:19 has design-service-life categories but is paywalled.
5. **Freeze–thaw damage rates** for concrete plinths, render or masonry in Vancouver. Only air-cycle counts (ECCC) were found, with no damage functions.
6. **Timber seat wear from human use** (abrasion, splinters) and **the effect of cleaning on surface growth**. Only general weathering erosion and BC Housing cleaning intervals were found.
7. **Solar irradiance on vertical façades by orientation for Vancouver.** Only horizontal sunshine hours were found. A future pass could use NRCan/CWEC weather files or NBC Appendix C if they contain this.
8. **Wind direction during rain at YVR** (rain-conditioned wind rose). ECCC normals give all-weather direction only, and the sources conflict between east (RDH) and south (BC Housing). Hourly ECCC data with an ISO 15927-3 analysis would resolve this.
9. **Short-duration rainfall intensities (IDF) for YVR** beyond the code's 15-minute value; ECCC IDF files were not retrieved.
10. **City of Vancouver Bulletin 2009-009** (rainscreen design and inspection for one- and two-family dwellings): blocked (403). It likely contains base-of-wall details and the inspection sequence.
