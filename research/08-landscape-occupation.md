### 0.1 Tags (same convention as 07)

- **Numbers** carry a round-bracket tag: `(S)` = SOURCED (the number is stated in the cited document), `(I)` = INFERRED (my calculation, a geometric consequence of the assembly, or practice I could not tie to a document; the reason is given next to it).
- **Performance claims** carry a square-bracket tag: `[S]` sourced, `[I]` inferred.
- Values from the brief are marked "(given)" and are counted separately.
- Clause, page, table and year numbers are references, not values, and are not tagged.
- "pdf p." is the page index of the PDF file; "printed p." is the page number printed on the page.

### H.2 Performance

#### (a) Water path
- Rain on the 2.55 m (I) of paving soaks through the joints into the bedding and base. New PICP infiltrates > 280 mm/h (S); MV23 designs for ≥ 28 mm/h (S) maintained — still close to Vancouver's 1-in-10 15-minute intensity of 40 mm/h (I) — so even a half-clogged surface rarely runs off; when it does, the 2 % (I) fall sends sheet flow to the outer edge and the bed [I].
- The 150 mm (S) base holds ≈ 60 mm (I) of rain (150 × 0.4 (S)). It drains into the subgrade where the soil allows, and otherwise along the 2 % (I) subgrade to the H3 underdrain and the rain garden [I].
- Near the house the subgrade slopes outward, so no base water flows toward the foundation drain; the inner curb is a partial cut-off [I].
- Interfaces: gutter overflow lands on the pad at d ≈ +770 to +900 (I) (J.2); downspout water must not cross the path (B651 8.2.4 [S]) — it is piped beneath (J12).
- Heat: impervious pavements reach 48–67 °C (S) at the surface in US summers; permeable pavements cool by evaporation when wet but can be hotter than impermeable ones when dry [S, EPA-CP]. Light-coloured pavers and summer shade from the tree reduce surface temperature at the bench (I).

#### (b) Root path
- Open-graded stone under pavers is moist and aerated, which roots favour; roots in compacted soil stay shallow [S, DAY10 for compaction; I for base]. Without a barrier, tree roots will enter the outer half of the base within 10–15 years and lift pavers (I).
- The I.1 root barrier along the outer curb holds roots out of the top ≈ 300 mm (S) (barriers redirect roots below their base; below 300 mm (S) there was no difference from controls after 7 years (S) [S, SMILEY09]; roots resurfaced within 1.2 m (S) beyond a 300 mm barrier [S, GILMAN96]). Expect some roots under the walking zone after 15–30 years (I). PICP is the only option here that can be lifted, root-pruned and relaid without demolition [I].

#### (c) People path
- Who uses it: walkers side by side, a person with a frame (≥ 900 mm (S)), a wheelchair user with a companion (1500 mm (S)) [S, IM pp.19–21]; a seated person's feet occupy the amenity zone.
- Wear: the foot zone in front of the seat is scuffed and loaded daily. Unpaved, trampled soil compacts in proportion to the logarithm of passages and loses cover [S, LGS75, search summary only] and would form a heel hollow that ponds at the bench front [I] — the main reason the pad and foot zone are paved.
- Trip and roll: settled or root-lifted pavers create edges; > 6 mm (S) needs a bevel and > 13 mm (S) must be re-laid [S, B651 Table 1].
- Lighting (if lit): ≥ 50 lx (S) at ground level along routes and rest areas [S, B651 8.2.9].

#### (d) Animal and insect path
- Paver joints host ants, mosses and small invertebrates; sunny, sandy joints may be used by ground-nesting bees (≈ 70 % (S) of native bees nest in the ground [S, AFN34]) — harmless and a reason to avoid sealing joints [I].
- Slugs shelter under the curb edges and in the shaded bench pad; birds forage on the path edge [I; S for slug refuges in moist shade, SAAN-SLUG].

#### (e) Failure modes

### I.2 Performance

#### (a) Water path — how soil moisture near the wall is managed
1. Roof water never reaches the soil within 5 m (S): piped to the rain garden (J).
2. Ground falls away: 5 % (given) over Z0/Z1, 2 % (I) over the pad and path, ≈ 11.5 % (I) over the bed.
3. The pad infiltrates only its own rain; its subgrade drains outward (H2).
4. **Moisture-holding layers are kept away from the wall:** no mulch within ≈ 0.6 m (I) of the stucco face (BCPG: 610–914 mm (S)); no irrigation or infiltrating beds within 5 m (S); low groundcover only (04 ev.eco.24: mulch and gravel keep soil moist [S]).
5. The wet zones (bed, rain garden) are ≥ 3.25 m (I) out; the bed's 450 mm (S) medium holds about 90 mm (S) of water, more than the Rain City design storm of 48 mm (S) falling on it [I].
6. **Shrinkable clay caveat:** if the site soil is clay (indicators: clay content > 25 % (S) and plasticity index > 30 % (S) mean very high shrink–swell potential [S, CBD184]), a tree drying the soil can move the foundation [S, 04 ev.eco.26/.27]. Ottawa keeps trees their mature height away on marine clay [S, 04 ev.eco.27]: 4.4 m (I) < 6 m (S) mature height → on clay, use a shrub-form *Amelanchier* or move the tree beyond 6 m (I).

#### (b) Root path at 5 / 15 / 30 years, and what stops it
- Root architecture: 80–90 % (S) of roots in the top 60 cm (S); 90–99 % (S) of root length in the top 1 m (S); roots seldom deeper than 2 m (S) [S, CROW05]. Lateral spread 1.7× (S) to > 3× (S) crown radius [S, CROW05 citing Gilman 1990]. For a 2 m (I) crown radius → roots 3.4–6 m (I) from the trunk.
- **Year 5:** roots fill the tree pit and enter the rain-garden medium (wettest, best-aerated soil) [I]; Bartens: tree roots raised infiltration in compacted soil [S].
- **Year 15:** roots that meet the barrier go under it at 300–450 mm (S) depth and resurface within ≈ 1.2 m (S) [S, GILMAN96]; below 300 mm (S) barriers make no difference [S, SMILEY09] → roots under the walking zone, possibly lifting pavers near the outer curb [I]. Roots approach 3.8 m (I) toward the drain zone.
- **Year 30:** fine roots can reach the drain envelope. Perforated plastic pipe is enterable through its perforations ("non-perforated pipe cannot be plugged" [S, OMAFRA]); roots are drawn to cool pipes by condensation [S, RANDRUP01]; geotextile is no barrier (ash roots crossed it within two years (S) [S, BARTENS08]); PVC joints are nearly root-resistant [S, ROLF94 abstract].
- **What stops them, in order of reliability [I]:**
  1. Distance plus a better water source: the rain garden 0.75 m from the trunk.
  2. A dry, compacted, low-organic backfill zone around the drain. Compacted soil restricts roots; root penetration fell as bulk density rose from 1.0 to 1.4 g/cm³ (S) [S, DAY10].
  3. The barrier, for the top 300 mm (S) only.
  4. Maintenance: camera and rodding from the 07 cleanouts.
- Danish data put 48 % (S) of intrusions within 3 m (S) of the tree and 44 % (S) at 3–6 m (S) [S, RANDRUP01]. Agricultural guidance keeps ordinary trees 15 m (S) from perforated drains that run for long periods [S, OMAFRA]; a house lot cannot meet that, so the tool should show **"roots at drain: possible from year 15–30"** for any tree within 6 m (I).

#### (c) Soil life — what it needs and where it lives
- Earthworms increase with organic matter and decrease with tillage and harmful chemicals; they need surface residue [S, NRCS-SBP]. They are more abundant in loam and clay loam than in sand or heavy clay [S]. They tolerate pH 4.5–8.2 (S) but not rapid change; temperatures above 29 °C (S) and freezing harm them; they need damp soil [S, WSU-EW].
- Mycorrhizal fungi are reduced by frequent tillage, broad-spectrum fungicides and very high N or P fertiliser [S, NRCS-SBP].
- Wood-chip mulch and leaf litter feed saprotrophic fungi and soil fauna and keep the bed surface moist [I; S for mulch moisture, 04 ev.eco.24].
- **Gradient across the section [I]:**

  | zone | soil life |
  |---|---|
  | Z0 gravel strip | almost none |
  | Z1 plinth strip | low (thin, dry, compacted below) |
  | Z2 pad and path | small fauna in the joints and the aerated base |
  | Z3 bed | high (organic, mulched, untilled) |
  | Z4 rain garden | high, seasonally wet |

- **Rule for the tool [I]:** soil life moves one step up for every 5 years of untilled mulch and leaf cover, and drops back after digging or soil replacement.

#### (d) People path
- People do not walk in the bed; maintenance is done from the path.
- The tree shades the bench and path in summer: a tree at its 6 m (S) upper height at d +4.4 m casts its noon shadow 1.2–2.9 m (I) toward the house at the 64° (I) solstice sun angle, covering the walking zone and reaching the bench on a south façade [I]. City guidance places benches under trees for shade [S, EDM 9.2.2.4].

### I.4 Conflicts between sources
1. **Tree to building:** 1.5 m (S) (By-law 9958 replacement trees) vs 2 m (S) columnar / 3 m (S) spreading (CoV-STG) vs 3 m (S) even for 6 m trees (Saanich) vs mature height on clay (S) (Ottawa via 04). By-law Schedule B's 2.0 m (S) for 20–30 cm (S) trunks is a counting rule, not a setback.
2. **Tree to drain:** 2.0 m (S) (EDM, sewer service connections) vs 2–3.5 m (S) (Adelaide, via RANDRUP01) vs ≤ 6 m (S) high-risk (Denmark) vs 15 m / 30 m (S) (OMAFRA, farm drains).
3. **Soil volume for a small tree:** 10 m³ (S) EDM; 8 m³ (S) Saanich (search summary only); ≈ 6.2 m³ (S) Lindsey & Bassuk for a 6.1 m (S) crown; 30 m³ (S) Toronto for any tree; 1000 mm × ≥ 10 m² (S) BCLS.
4. **Bed medium depth and organics:** 300–450 mm (S) BCLS; 450 mm (S) MV23; 400 mm (S) West Vancouver; organics 10–20 % (S) BCLS, 15 % (S) MV23, 25–30 % (S) MMCD (via COOK16), 5–10 % (S) City street-tree mix.
5. **Mulch depth:** 50–100 mm (S) ISA vs 100–150 mm (S) WSU vs 70–90 mm (S) City street trees.
6. **Mulch-free band at the wall:** "narrow" (WSU) vs 610–914 mm (S) (BCPG) vs a 914 mm (S) weed-free zone with a 610 mm (S) gravel strip (UNL). The given 300 mm (given) gravel strip is narrower than all numeric values; Z1 without mulch extends the mulch-free band to ≈ 590 mm (I) from the stucco face.
7. **Root barriers:** a 300 mm (S) barrier with roots resurfacing within 1.2 m (S) (Gilman) vs a 450 mm (S) barrier with no effect below 300 mm (S) (Smiley); City: barriers at all new street trees (EDM 2026) vs only within 2 m (S) of hardscape (STG 2011).
8. **Tillage:** IRMP16 "aerate or till surface 25 mm (S) deep between plants each spring" vs NRCS/WSU: tillage destroys burrows and mycorrhizae [S].
9. **04 ev.eco.25** (roots exploit existing defects, sound pipe resists) holds for jointed sewers; for perforated drains OMAFRA shows the perforations themselves are entry points [S] — add to 04.

### J.2 Performance

#### (a) Water path: what happens in a storm, from roof to sewer

1. **Roof → gutter.** Design input: Vancouver 15-min 1-in-10 rainfall **10 mm (S)** (V1 Table C-2, pdf p.934), i.e. 40 mm/h (I). The code accepts that small roofs see this exceeded more often than once in 10 years because their concentration time is under 15 min [S, V1 Appendix C pdf p.927]. One-day 1-in-50 rain: 112 mm (S) today, 117 mm (S) 2020s, 127 mm (S) 2050s.
2. **Gutter overflow.** With the front lip lower than the back [S, FHB], overflow leaves the gutter as a sheet at the lip (d ≈ +770 (given)) and lands at d ≈ +770 to +900 (I, falling ≈ 3 m with low horizontal velocity) — **on the rear 0–110 mm of the bench seat** (seat from +790 (given)) and then the paved bench pad (H). Overflowing eavestroughs "may result in water backing up and overflowing against adjacent building walls" [S, BCH-MM02]; CMHC: overflow "can run down the building and pool against the foundation" [S, CMHC13]. Cause in practice: debris, not undersizing [S, CMHC13; BCH-MM02].
3. **Downspout → boot → pipe.** All roof water leaves the wall zone in a sealed pipe; nothing infiltrates within 5 m (S). This is what the two-pipe rule and the 5 m setback are for: "to limit harm … from excessive moisture loading on foundation walls … and the soil immediately beneath footings" [S, Note A-9.14.5.3.(2)].
4. **Rain garden.** Water enters over a rock apron, ponds up to 200 mm (S), infiltrates through 450 mm (S) of medium into 300 mm (S) of rock and the native soil, and drains down within 48 h (S) preferred, 72 h (S) max (MV23). In Vancouver's till the subdrain will carry much of it (I).
5. **Overflow → detention tank → storm.** When ponding reaches the crest, the grated riser takes the excess to the small-site detention tank and the storm sewer [S, B2 2.4.2.4–2.4.2.5]. If the riser is blocked, water rises 100 mm (S) more and spills over the far rim, away from the house (I).

**Hydraulic rule that sets the levels [I]:** the pipe outlet must sit at least 50 mm (S, MV23 inlet drop) above the overflow crest, or the garden backs water up the pipe toward the wall at every full storm (BCH-SFD: systems should "overflow before backing up" [S]). With a 1 % (S) pipe that must pass under the path base (crown ≈ −226 (I) at the path outer edge, below the bedding), the outlet reaches the garden at ≈ −355 (I); the crest must therefore be ≤ −405 (I) and the rim ≤ −305 (I). That is why the planting bed between path and rain garden falls **≈ 11.5 % (I)** instead of the 2 % (S) minimum (MV23 planter figure "2 % min. away from foundation"). The surface rill (J16) avoids this constraint.

**Sizing rule of thumb.** Three sources give different kinds of number:
- MV23 Table 4-A: maximum impervious : rain-garden base area **30:1 (S)** for single-family roof water → base ≥ 3.5 m² (I) for 106 m². This is a loading cap, not a capture size.
- CNV5 sizing table (default soil 10 mm/h (S)): 200 m² roof → 22 m² (S); 250 → 30 m² (S); 300 → 36 m² (S): about **11–12 % of roof area (I)**; at 50 mm/h (S): 10–14 m² (S) ≈ 5 % (I).
- STEP-BR: impervious : footprint 5:1–15:1 (S) on poorly draining soils.
- **Adopted for the tool: ≈ 10 % of the contributing roof area (I)** → ≈ 10 m² (I) for 106 m² (e.g. 2.5 m × 4 m (I)); test the soil and adjust.

#### (b) Root path
- Rain-garden plants root through the 450 mm (S) medium into the rock; woody plants send roots into the perforated subdrain over time [I; ev.eco.25 roots exploit openings]. Placing the subdrain near the top of the rock (S) keeps it accessible for rodding (I).
- The tree beside the garden (I) will root heavily into the garden medium, the wettest, best-aerated soil in the section [I]. That helps infiltration: in a container trial, tree roots in compacted clay loam raised infiltration by an average of 153 % (S), and green ash roots "penetrated both the geotextile and the subsoil within two years" (S, two years) [S, BARTENS08]. The same finding means **geotextile does not stop roots** — relevant to the foundation drain (I.3).

#### (c) People path
- The pipe is invisible; people meet the system at the boot (cleanout cap), the splash pad, and the rain-garden rim. Rim drop ≈ 300 mm (I) is below the 600 mm (S) at which a guard is required (V2 9.8.8.1.(1), pdf p.30) [S]; a 3:1 (I) slope is walkable and plantable.
- Ponding of 200 mm (S) for up to 48–72 h (S) is a shallow-water hazard for toddlers only during and after storms [I]; no source sets a depth limit for private rain gardens.
- Do not let path or rain-garden water run onto public sidewalks (B2 Note A-2.4.2.4.(2)) [S].

#### (d) Animal and insect path
- Standing water must drain within 48 h (S) preferred [S, MV23]; municipal rain-garden FAQs say mosquito larvae need 3–7 days (S) of standing water (MOSQ, search summary only), so a draining garden is not a breeding site [I].
- Wet-zone sedges and rushes, rim shrubs (*Ribes sanguineum* flowers early for hummingbirds and bees, *Symphoricarpos albus* and *Rosa nutkana* fruit for birds) make the garden the richest habitat strip in the section [I; species list S, CNV5].
- Rodents use rock-filled reservoirs and overflow pipes as runs if the riser grate is missing (I).

### J.4 Conflicts between sources
1. **Setback from the house:** 3 m (S) "downslope of building foundations, and only in areas where foundations have footing drains" (MV23 §4.3; CoV IRMP16 p.II-9; CNV5) vs **5 m (S)** (VBBL 9.14.5.3.(2) and Note, which names rain gardens explicitly; BCH-SFD quotes the 5 m). VBBL governs in Vancouver. UK guidance uses the same 5 m (S) from buildings for soakaways [UK-SOAK, search summary only]. [04: ev.eco.28] (3 m) should carry a "not in Vancouver" note.
2. **Ponding depth:** MV23 150–300 mm (S), 200 typical (S); CoV IRMP16 150–300 mm (S); CNV5 150–200 mm (S); CoV EDM-GI ≤ 150 mm (S) in a 48 mm storm (streets only).
3. **Drawdown:** MV23 48 h (S) preferred / 72 h (S) max; EDM-GI 24 h (S) ponded, 72 h (S) soil; IRMP16 72 h (S); STEP-BR 48 h (S).
4. **Medium depth and organics:** 450 mm (S) (MV23, CNV) vs 300 mm (S) min to 1200 mm (S) desirable (IRMP16); organics 15–25 % (S) (MV23 spec), 15–20 % (S) (CNV), 10–25 % (S) (BCGB02), 3–5 % (S) by dry weight for tree-trench soil (STEP-TT).
5. **Medium infiltration rate:** 70 mm/h (S) (MV23 rain garden) vs 50 mm/h (S) (MV23 absorbent landscape; IRMP16).
6. **Sizing:** 30:1 cap (S) (MV23) vs ≈ 11 % of roof (I from CNV5 table) vs 10 % (S) of lot area for infiltration (BCGB02 pdf p.144) vs I:P 5–15 (S) (STEP).
7. **Pipe size:** CNV 100 mm (S) solid leader pipe vs MV23 "PVC, DR 35, 150 mm min." (S) (that minimum is for subdrains); STEP underdrain ≥ 200 mm (S) (larger municipal systems).
8. **Downspout extension where discharged at grade:** CMHC09 ≥ 1,200 mm (S) vs BCH-SFD "past the backfill zone" (S) vs VBBL no number (S). In this design nothing discharges at grade except the boot relief.
9. **Grading:** BCH-SFD ≥ 2 % (S) for 3 m (S) or past the backfill; CMHC98 10 % (S) for the first 2 m (S); brief 5 % (given) for 3 m.
10. **Rain garden vs tank:** B2 2.4.2.4.(2) permits disconnection with overflow to storm; 2.4.2.5.(4) still requires the tank on small sites (S). The City's GRI typology encourages disconnection (S). No document read says whether a rain garden earns any tank-size credit (open question).

## L. Animals and insects

### L.0 Principle [I]
**Exclusion at the envelope, habitat away from it.** The wall, eave, gravel strip and plinth strip (d < +700) are an exclusion zone: screened, dry, open and inspectable. The bed, tree and rain garden (d ≥ +3250) are the habitat zone. The paved pad and path between them are a crossing zone used by ground insects, birds and people. Every habitat source read places nests, boxes, mulch and dense planting **away from** the house: bat houses preferably not on homes [S, BCBAT25]; mulch-free band 610–914 mm (S) [S, BCPG]; shrubs ≥ 610 mm (S) from buildings [S, UCIPM]; branches not touching the house [S, BC carpenter-ant page].

## Open questions

1. **Site soil and groundwater** (governs H3, I.2(a), J1–J3): infiltration test at the rain-garden and path locations (MV23 needs ≥ 13 mm/h (S) for full infiltration), seasonal high water table (test pit in February), clay content and plasticity index (CBD184 trigger for the clay tree rule), direction of perched groundwater relative to the house.
2. **Rain garden vs detention tank:** does a rain garden reduce the VBBL 2.4.2.5 small-site tank (3,400–7,200 L (S))? No document read gives a credit. Where is the tank, and does the perimeter-drain sump also discharge through it?
3. **VBBL Figure A-9.14.5.3.(2):** the figure (viewed) dimensions only the 5 m building setbacks, the 0.9 m firefighter access and the 4.9 m laneway separation; the hatched band along property lines is not dimensioned. Confirm the property-line setback with the City.
4. **Section extent:** at 6 m the section cuts the rain garden's house-side slope; extend to ≈ 8 m (I) to show the whole garden, or show it cut.
5. **Exactly 2 % cross fall** (accessibility ≤ 1:50 vs drainage ≥ 2 %): is the house path to be treated as an accessible route (B651 8.2) or a visitable-dwelling route (B651 7.3.1.1: 1000 mm (S) wide)? The latter would allow a narrower path and a steeper fall.
6. **Heel space and leg projection:** no sourced anthropometric numbers. Panero & Zelnik, Neufert, BS 8300-1 and ISO 21542 were not accessible; values in K5 and K9 are inferred.
7. **Bench structure** after moving the front support (K6): moment connection at a single pier, or an elongated footing — needs a structural check (06 C8 already flagged self-bracing).
8. **Insect-screen aperture:** confirm ≤ 2 mm with an envelope consultant and check vent free area (cavity bulletin requires the cavity ≥ 80 % (S) open; screens reduce flow).
9. **Root intrusion into residential foundation drains:** no peer-reviewed study found. The tool's year-15–30 "roots possible" state rests on sewer and farm-drain data.
10. **Species choice:** *Amelanchier* is in the same family as the worst sewer intruder in the Swedish data (*Malus*); no intrusion data for *Amelanchier* or *Acer circinatum*.
11. **Gravel strip width vs mulch-free band:** 300 mm (given) strip vs 610–914 mm (S) recommended band; Z1 without mulch gives ≈ 590 mm (I). Does the architect accept groundcover in Z1, or should the strip widen to 610 mm?
12. **ASHRAE 55 "occupied zone" definition** (base standard not read) and the floor temperature at the slab edge (needs the 2-D model in 07 open question 9).
13. **Path runoff as "directed water"** (only matters for the concrete option H-c): does the City treat a path draining onto a bed within 5 m as an infiltration feature?
