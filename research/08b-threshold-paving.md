# 08b — Addendum: junction M, permeable paving at a flush sliding/folding-door threshold (0–6 m out)

Researched: 2026-10-13. **New brief:** at this section the wall becomes a large sliding/folding glazed wall with a near-flush accessible threshold opening straight onto exterior permeable pavers with a shrub, facing east or west. The sliding wall and threshold product are in `11-sliding-wall.md` and the ground floor/slab in `12-ground-floor.md` (other researchers). - The gravel strip (07 F), the plinth strip Z1 and bench pad (08 I/H) are replaced across the door opening by a paved terrace.
- The rainwater path (08 J), the bed and tree (08 I) and the rain garden at ≥ 5 m are kept.

**Reading [I]:** the threshold geometry is not code-governed for a house. B651 (13 mm (S)) is the tighter benchmark and is adopted. Two code rules do bite: 9.15.4.6 (150 mm (S)), and the 5 m (S) rule as soon as any water is *directed* into the ground near the house.

## M.2 Is permeable paving within 5 m an "infiltration system"?

1. VBBL's note lists trenches, swales, basins, rain gardens, soak-away pits and proprietary units — systems that receive *directed* water — and excludes landscaping [S].
2. The City's GI manual says permeable pavement should manage only the rain falling on it [S, EDM-GI].
3. STEP: no setback for permeable pavement without run-on; 4 m (S) down-gradient if it receives drainage from other surfaces; "a smaller setback may be acceptable where foundations are protected by a geomembrane" [S, STEP-PP].
4. ICPI: PICP should be down-slope of foundations with piped footing drains; an impermeable liner or waterproofing on basement walls against PICP is recommended; the residential detail applies a waterproof membrane vertically against the house foundation; checklist: ≥ 3 m (10 ft (S)) from foundation walls that have **no** waterproofing or drainage [S, ICPI TS18 pdf pp.5, 12, 16].
5. UK: infiltration devices ≥ 5 m (S) from buildings (Approved Document H §3.25a). Permeable paving may be used with an impermeable barrier under the storage layer; a "Type A" full-infiltration pavement that takes only its own rain needs no tanking membrane within 5 m; "Outlets should be located more than 5 m away from the walls of a building" [S, MARSH21, quoting ADH].

**Conclusion [I]:**
- PICP that receives only its own rainfall is not a directed infiltration system and may infiltrate inside 5 m.
- The strip at the door **is** different. It receives run-off from the glass (directed water), sits over the foundation backfill and drain-rock zone (to +585 (given)), and any saturation of its base reaches the threshold and slab edge directly.
- So that strip is lined and drained. The lined zone ends at **+1850 (I)**, covering the 1700 mm (S) level landing plus the channel.
- The water collected there is piped to the rain garden ≥ 5 m (S) away, matching UK practice for outlets.
- If the City reads VBBL more strictly, the liner extends to +5000 (I) (open question 2).

## M.4 Performance

### (a) Water path — roof, glass, landing, channel, pipe
1. **Roof:** the gutter and leader (08 J15, J14) take roof water to the rain garden. Gutter overflow falls at the lip (+770 (given)), on the landing about 0.6 m out from the door (I) — a reason for the channel and the 2 % fall [I].
2. **Overhang shelter:** a 600 mm (given) overhang fully shelters only the top 150 mm (S) of the wall (O/4 rule [03: ev.assembly.overhang-code-quarter-rule]). The sill, about 2.4 m (I) below the eave, gets full wind-driven rain. ADM1 would want the landing covered ≥ 600 mm (S) deep — the overhang reaches that depth, but it does not keep driving rain off the threshold [I].
3. **Wind-driven rain on the glass:** glass absorbs nothing, so everything that hits it runs down to the sill [I].
   - Estimate with the BSD-148 semi-empirical formula [03: ev.assembly.wdr-semi-empirical]: 0.22 (S) × 6 m/s (I, storm wind) × 40 mm/h (I, the 10 mm (S) 1-in-10 15-min rain) × RDF 0.5 (S) × height factor 0.7 (S) × shelter 0.5 (S) ≈ **9 mm/h (I)** on the façade.
   - Over 2.4 m (I) of exposed glass that is ≈ 22 L/h per metre of door (I).
   - **Orientation:** in Vancouver the main wind-driven rain direction is east, with 3× (S) the driving rain of south faces [S, 03 ev.climate.wdr-direction]; west was not measured [S]. An east-facing threshold is the wet case; a west-facing one is the hot, dry case for the shrub [I].
4. **Rain on the landing:** 1.85 m (I) × 40 mm/h (I) ≈ 74 L/h per metre (I). With the glass run-off, ≈ 0.03 L/s per metre of door (I) — trivial for a 100 mm channel and a 100 mm pipe at 1 % (≈ 7.5 L/s (I), 08 J12).
5. **Paths through the landing:**
   - Surface water: the 2 % (I) fall away plus the channel at the door, so water never runs toward the sill [S principle, GG47].
   - Water through the joints: bedding and base → liner → underdrain → outlet (I).
   - Bedding water at the door: the channel's sub-surface inlets [S, ACO-SD].
   - Nothing enters the backfill or drain rock under the landing [I].
6. **Unlined terrace:** infiltrates its own rain (new > 280 mm/h (S), maintained ≥ 28 mm/h (S) [S, MV23]); overflow to its edge underdrain if the soil is tight (I).
7. **Where it all ends:** solid pipe → rain garden ≥ 5 m (S) → overflow → small-site detention tank → storm [S, B2] (08 J).
8. **Splash onto glass and track:** rain on hard paving splashes up to about 300 mm (S) (German splash zone [03: ev.assembly.splash-zone-de]). The grate directly in front of the sill catches drops at the door line and reduces splash onto the track (I). The lowest 300 mm (I) of glass and the track will streak and collect grit: add to cleaning (I).
9. **Water at the sill itself (interface with 11):**
   - Vancouver's driving-rain wind pressure is 160 Pa (S) [V1 Table C-2]. That equals ≈ 16 mm (I) of water head (pressure/10, as used in 06).
   - A threshold of ≤ 13–15 mm (S) cannot hold that back by height alone.
   - The door therefore needs a drained, pressure-equalised sill draining **into the channel**, and the channel must never back up (I). This is the strongest reason for continuous maintenance of the grate and silt box [I].

### (b) Root path
- *Vaccinium ovatum* roots fill the 0.86 m³ (I) pocket within about 5 years (I), then explore the moist, aerated unlined PICP base around it (I). Most roots stay shallow (trees: 80–90 % (S) in the top 60 cm (S) [S, CROW05]).
- Toward the house, roots meet the HDPE barrier and the liner upturn at +1850. They pass under a barrier only below its base [S, GILMAN96/SMILEY09], and here the base is 600 mm (I) down, below the underdrain → **no root path into the lined landing, the underdrain perforations (an entry point [S, OMAFRA]) or the threshold slab** (I).
- The foundation drain sits 1.3–1.8 m (I) from the pocket beneath the lined zone. A small shrub is a low-risk source (I); the 08 I tree remains the main root risk.

### (c) People path
- **Arrival and turning:**
  - Level, firm landing ≥ 1700 × 1700 mm (S) at the door — turning in front of a wide opening.
  - Sliding-door manoeuvring areas ≤ 1350 mm (S) deep fit inside it.
  - No cross-fall at the door [S, GG47: cross-falls make manual wheelchairs drift].
- **Crossing the threshold:**
  - Rise ≤ 13 mm (S), bevelled above 6 mm (S).
  - Grate flush with the pavers; slots ≤ 6.5 mm (S) and perpendicular to travel, which traps neither heels (heel-resistant limit 8 mm (S)) nor castors or canes (13 mm (S)).
- **Surface:** joints ≤ 6 mm (S) in the landing; wet BPN ≥ 55 (S). Smooth and uninterrupted; anti-slip treatments unobtrusive [S, GG47].
- **Terrace use:**
  - The 1.4 m (I) unlined strip beyond the landing plus the landing itself (when the door is open) take a small table and chairs (I).
  - B651 asks for a level firm surface ≥ 2000 mm (S) around a fixed picnic table [S]; that only fits on the full 3.25 m (I) depth if the shrub is placed to one side of the opening (plan decision) (I).
  - Narrow joints keep chair legs from catching (I).
- **Folding doors:** if the leaves stack or swing outward, their swing zone must stay clear of furniture and the shrub (open question, 11) (I).
- **Wear:** the strip within 1 m (I) of the door carries every passage; joints there lose aggregate first (I).

### (d) Animal and insect path
- **Grates:** slots ≤ 6.5 mm (S) exclude rats (which pass ≈ 13 mm (S) gaps) but not every mouse (which passes > 6 mm (S) holes) [S, BC-RAT; UCIPM]. The pipe network is a rodent route between the rain garden and the channel: screen the rain-garden pipe outlet (ICPI uses 16-mesh screen (S)) or fit a flap (I), and keep the silt-box lid tight (I).
- **Door track:** seal all gaps > 6 mm (S) at the sill ends and jambs [S, BC-RAT]; ants use sill cavities (I).
- **Shrub:** *Vaccinium* flowers feed bees April–August (S) [S, P2] and its berries feed birds (I). The bees are a few metres from the door; huckleberry bees are not aggressive (I). Lowest branches kept 150–200 mm (S) up so rats find no cover [S, BC-RAT].
- **Channel water:**
  - Slugs shelter in the moist channel and silt box (I).
  - A silt bucket holding water for more than a week can breed mosquitoes. Municipal FAQs: larvae need 3–7 days (S) of standing water, and gutters breed more mosquitoes than rain gardens (search summary only, MOSQ). Keep the outlet free-draining (I).

## M.6 Conflicts between sources
1. **Threshold height:** ≤ 13 mm (S) (B651, VBBL 3.8) vs ≤ 15 mm (S) total (ADM1, GG47).
2. **Bevel trigger:** > 6 mm (S) (B651) vs > 5 mm (S) (ADM1, GG47).
3. **Landing slope:** ≤ 1:50 (S) cross slope (B651) vs a 1:40–1:60 (S) fall (GG47) vs "level" ≤ 1:60 (S) (ADM1) vs drainage ≥ 2 % (S) (BC Housing). 2 % (I) satisfies B651, GG47 and BC Housing, but not ADM1's "level".
4. **Grate openings:** ≤ 6.5 mm (S) (ACO heel-safe) vs ≤ 8 mm (S) (ASME heel-resistant) vs ≤ 13 mm (S) (B651, VBBL, ADA) vs ≤ 18 mm (S) (GG47).
5. **Liner material and extent:**
   - Material: 30 mil (S) PVC (MN-PP) vs 30 mil (S) polyethylene (Boston) vs ≥ 40 mil (S) HDPE (Minnesota liner levels) vs "HDPE, EPDM, PVC" (ICPI).
   - Extent: vertical membrane only (ICPI residential detail) vs vertical + 305 mm (S) under the base (Boston) vs a full liner under the storage layer (ADH §2.9 via MARSH21) vs none for own-rain Type A paving (MARSH21; STEP).
6. **Distance from foundations:** 3 m (S) without waterproofing/drainage (ICPI) vs 4 m (S) with run-on (STEP) vs 5 m (S) for directed infiltration (VBBL; ADH).
7. **Foundation clearance vs level threshold:** VBBL 9.15.4.6 ≥ 150 mm (S) vs B651 7.4.7's level threshold to patios and ≤ 13 mm (S) rise.
8. **Wide joints:** STEP allows ≤ 15 mm (S) joints for pedestrian PICP; the City toolkit says wide-joint pavers should not be used on routes for disabled users [S]; Ottawa 6 mm (S) preferred / 10 mm (S) max → landing ≤ 6 mm (S), terrace ≤ 10 mm (S).
9. **Shrub light:** E-Flora "shade-intolerant" [S] vs P2 "sun to partial shade" [S] for *Vaccinium*.

## M.7 Open questions
1. **FFL vs exterior grade (12):** sets the terrace level, the bed slope to the rain garden and the pipe levels (08 J hydraulic rule).
2. **City interpretation:** is own-rain PICP within 5 m an "infiltration system"? If yes, extend the liner to +5000 (I) and drain the whole terrace to the rain garden.
3. **VBBL 9.15.4.6** at a near-flush threshold: will the City accept a lined, drained landing as a constructed surface rather than "finished ground"? "Finished ground" is undefined (01 U3).
4. **Sill product (11):** sill height and bevel, internal drainage outlet into the channel, NAFS water-penetration rating for an east façade at 160 Pa (S); how the liner turn-up and channel haunch tie to the sill pan.
5. **Folding vs sliding:** outward-folding leaves need a clear swing and stack zone on the landing.
6. **Channel product:** width, depth, load class, outlet size, availability in Canada (UK/US data used).
7. **Rodent/mosquito details** at the silt box and rain-garden pipe outlet (screen vs flap valve).
8. ***Vaccinium ovatum* root habit:** no source read on whether it suckers; spacing from the underdrain rests on the barrier.
9. **Liner durability** buried for 30+ years: no source read.
