## 0.1 Answer in one place

**Recommended edge character: A, "black timber, grey ground".** Vertical charred and oiled board-on-board sits above a recessed, pigmented mineral plinth that welcomes moss. The base flashing drips inside the shadow under the boards. The larch soffit is light, every piece of metal is one dark colour, and there is no visible zinc or galvanized line. A 600 mm stone/gravel band runs between the wall and the bench.

## 0.2 What the v2 edge looks like from outside (baseline) [I, from the model geometry]

- **Base.** A person standing outside sees, from the ground up:
  - About 220 mm of grey cement parge.
  - Under the stucco, a 46 mm deep recess in shadow (stucco face x ≈ −109; plinth face x = −63).
  - The flashing nose and its 7 mm kick, 12 mm proud of the stucco at about +207 to +214.
  - A 10 mm open gap.
  - The zinc casing bead at +230, then the stucco.
- **What this does well.** The recessed plinth already gives a good shadow line.
- **What spoils it.** Two bright metal lines (flashing nose and zinc bead) run only 10–20 mm apart at shin height. They catch splash dirt. Zinc run-off also suppresses moss directly below (UWML-MOSS), which works against the "moss welcome" brief.
- **Eave.** It reads as three stacked bands:
  - a 104 mm metal edge band at the top,
  - a 108 mm gutter front,
  - about 220 mm of larch fascia below the gutter, over a 516 mm larch soffit.
  The visible roof edge is about 450 mm deep, roughly 15 % of the 3.1 m wall. That reads heavy unless it is made into one deliberate band.

### 1.1 What the precedents agree on [I unless tagged]

1. **The base is a different, harder material, and the wall overhangs it.** Seen in P1, P2, P6 and P8, and as a hard skirt in P11. The shadow under the overhang does the visual work. A metal flashing is never the thing you see.
2. **Timber is left to weather only where the architecture protects it.** P5 is "protected by the architecture" [S] HK-LUD. Where it is not protected, it is stained black (P2) or charred (P4, P10), which hides uneven weathering.
3. **Mineral surfaces are allowed to erode and colonise, but in a controlled pattern.** Brick erosion checks (P7), poured strata (P8), raised plaster joints (P11).
4. **Living edges overhang softly.** Plants spill over the roof edge (P12, VREF-7a4a) or the roof runs down to meet the ground (P3).
5. **Gutterless eaves need a stone band where the water lands.** Ditches up to about 1 m wide [S] JAANUS-AMA.

## 2. Finish options and how they weather in Vancouver

### 2.0 Vancouver drivers and the zones of this edge

- **Climate** (via 03):
  - About 1,153 mm of rain a year, on 165 rain days.
  - East is the main wind-driven-rain direction (RDH).
  - Under a 500 mm overhang, an east wall received about 3 % of horizontal rainfall. With no overhang, 3 m below the roof, it received about 13 %.
  - Overhangs reduce rain deposition by about 50 % [S] BSD-013.
- **Algae** concentrate on north and west faces near trees (04 ev.eco.08).
- **Greying.** Uncoated cladding greys within months in high rainfall [S] ABODO-WEA.
- **Zones used in the timelines below** (so the decades view can colour them separately):
  - **U, under-eave band** (top roughly 150–600 mm of wall). Sheltered from rain and UV, so timber keeps its original colour [S] NAK-LONG, and dirt is not washed off [S] EQ-CLEAN.
  - **F, wall field.**
  - **B, base splash band** (lowest roughly 150–300 mm of cladding). Weathers fastest from splash-back [snippet] SB-PRE. Charred boards silver first here, where they stay wet longest [S] NAK-LONG.
  - **P, plinth.**
  - **G, ground.**
  - **M, metal and its run-off shadow.**

### 2.3 Dark vs light

- **Dark finishes heat up.** Dark coatings raise board temperature even on low-sun days [snippet] via 02 ev.general.timber.dark-coating-heat.
- **Dark finishes need more frequent renewal.** Thermory gives black semi-transparent paint about 5 yr and opaque black about 7 yr, against 10–15 yr for other opaque paints [S] THERM-IG.
- **Charred black is the exception** because no film is involved: alkyd-oiled char is the most colour-stable option on Nakamoto's scale [S] NAK-GEN. On south and west faces, dark surfaces also dry faster after rain. I found no field number for Vancouver [I].
- **Light renders show green algae (north) and dirt streaks most.** Dark moulds favour south faces [S] JOH11.
- **Dark timber hides mould and differential weathering** but shows pale dust, pollen and efflorescence from mineral surfaces above it [I]. In this edge nothing mineral sits above the timber, so that risk does not arise.
- **In a palette that ages well in rain,** dark should sit where water and moss would otherwise stain (wall field, metals), mid-grey where moss is welcome (plinth), and light only where sheltered (soffit) [I].

### 3.2 Base flashing: three ways to handle it

- **(a) Hidden "drip in the shadow" (recommended for timber, Character A).**
  - The flashing's sloped leg stops ≈10 mm past the **plinth** face. That satisfies the code's ≥5 mm from the face below [S] via 01, and CMHC's ≥10 mm, read against the plinth.
  - The boards overhang it by 40–60 mm and end 20–25 mm higher, so water on the board faces drips off the bevelled board ends onto gravel beyond the plinth face. It never touches the flashing.
  - Only cavity water uses the flashing.
  - Finish: charcoal PVDF, with a short 6 mm 45° kick.
  - From standing eye height (looking down at roughly 30–40° from 2 m away) the nose sits inside a 23 mm tall, 51–70 mm deep shadow band. It reads as a dark line, not as metal [I].
  - This departs from 06's "≥10–13 mm past the cladding face", which came from guides, not the code. It needs reviewer sign-off (§5 C5).
- **(b) Colour-matched minimal (for stucco or render, Character B).**
  - The nose sits 10 mm past the render face, with a 6 mm kick, in the plinth colour.
  - Drop the separate casing-bead line, or align the bead's nose with the flashing, so the eye sees one line, not two [I].
  - No bare zinc.
- **(c) Expressed.** A deliberate 30–50 mm folded "kick plate" band in dark bronze. It reads as a skirting, but collects splash dirt and grit. Not recommended here [I].

### 3.3 How the cladding stops above the plinth

1. **Boards stop clear of the plinth** and project 51–70 mm beyond its face. They are bevelled 15° with end grain sealed [S] ABODO-DET/RC-PRE.
2. **The insect mesh is hidden.** Use a perforated stainless or aluminium profile (06 A14), powder-coated black. Set it 10 mm above the board ends, so it sits behind the board line and in shadow. The open area must be ≥ the cavity's free area (06 A14) [I].
3. **The lowest horizontal batten** sits within about 75 mm of the board ends, so the board ends cannot flap or cup [I].
4. **Band B is a known wet band.** Re-oil it on a shorter cycle. Designing a replaceable lowest course (Lunawood suggests horizontal boards where clearance must be reduced, replaceable when necessary [S] LUNA-NA) is an option, but it breaks the vertical rhythm, so A does not use it [I].

### 3.4 Fasteners: concealed or expressed

- **Board-on-board hides most fasteners.**
  - Under-boards: one central row [I].
  - Over-boards: two fixings that land in the 100 mm gap between under-boards, so no fastener passes through two boards [S] RC-PRE (fix each piece independently).
  - On the over-boards the fasteners show as two stainless dots per batten line. Align them on level lines so they read as intended [I].
- **Charred faces:** use black-headed or char-coloured stainless heads so they do not glint [I].
- **Never use galvanized fasteners in WRC or oak** (tannin stains; corrosion) [S] TRADA-CU; [S] 06 C.

### 3.5 Corners

- **Timber:** butt-lap rather than mitre [S] TRADA-CU.
  - On the more exposed face (east/south/west), let the over-board run past and cover the end grain of the return face's last board. Leave an 8–10 mm clearance at board ends [S] TRADA-CU.
  - Alternative: a recessed dark metal angle as a "shadow corner" [I].
- **Plinth:** a 10 mm chamfered arris on the parge. It chips less, and reads crisply in raking light [I].
- **Eave corner:** carry the edge metal round in one folded piece, with the gutter's end cap in the same colour [I].

### 3.6 Eave: soffit vents, fascia and gutter

- **Soffit vent concealment.**
  - Keep v2's 20 mm cavity-top slot under the frieze [S] 06 B22, but make the mesh black and set it ≥10 mm inside, so the slot reads as a shadow line between the dark wall and the light soffit [I].
  - Replace the separate soffit mesh strip (`soffit-mesh`) with 6–8 mm open joints between soffit boards over black mesh, so the vent disappears into the board rhythm [I].
- **Soffit finish.** Larch with a clear UV-pigmented oil. It is sheltered, so it keeps its honey tone (zone U) [S, by analogy] NAK-LONG.
  - It collects cobwebs and dirt, because rain never washes it [S] EQ-CLEAN.
  - Plan a gentle wash at the twice-yearly roof check [S] BCH-MM02 via 06.
- **Fascia: make the edge one band.** Use the same dark colour for edge metal, gutter and fascia, either charred boards matching the wall or a charcoal metal cover [I].
- **Optional thinner eave** (structure and roofing to confirm) [I]:
  - In v2 the polyiso runs out to x = −460, and four nailer plies (162 mm) build the edge up to +3516. That is why the fascia is 446 mm deep.
  - Stop the insulation near the wall line, at about x −100, outside the 50 mm wall insulation. Taper the roof over the overhang at roughly 20–25 % down to a single 38 mm nailer at the edge.
  - Visible fascia: soffit +3081 to roof edge ≈ +3400, so ≈ 320 mm instead of 446 mm.
  - It also saves the polyiso over an unheated overhang, which belongs to the carbon file to weigh.
- **Gutter options.**

### 3.7 How the bench and ground meet the wall

- **Keep the wall foot clean and mineral.** A gravel band runs about 600 mm from the plinth face, roughly to the eave line, with steel edging (07 F3). It is the *amaochi* idea [S] JAANUS-AMA, sized [I].
- **Beyond it:** moss, low planting and leaf litter as far as the bench. The bench stands in the living zone, not against the wall. This keeps the ≥200 mm cladding clearance free of soil creep (07) [I].
- **Bench piers** in the same pigmented concrete as the plinth parge, with chamfered tops at ≥ +152 [S] via 06. The pier tops will carry moss and lichen, so the oak seat must stay clear of them by its 25 mm stand-off [S] via 06.
- **Oak silvers to grey.** That matches the charcoal-and-silver palette. Keep zinc away from it: oak is incompatible with zinc [S] VMZ-COMP.
- **No paving against the wall.** Where a path must reach the door, stop it at the gravel band. Paving raises splash, and Lunawood's clearance rises to 300 mm above impermeable surfaces [S] LUNA-NA.

### 3.8 Palette and metals

- **Five colours that converge rather than clash with age** [I]:
  - charcoal: charred boards and all metal;
  - warm dark grey: plinth parge and piers, with algae and moss green arriving from about year 5;
  - honey: sheltered larch soffit;
  - silver: oak bench;
  - grey-green: gravel, stone and moss.
- **Metals:**
  - One PVDF colour (charcoal) for base flashing, edge metal, gutter and downspouts.
  - No bare zinc, galvanized or copper upslope of the moss zones: their run-off suppresses moss [S] UWML-MOSS; via 04.
  - Zinc is also incompatible with WRC and oak run-off [S] VMZ-COMP.
  - No aluminium against parge or stucco [S] via 06.
  - Stainless fasteners [S] RC-PRE.

### 4.2 Character B, "Lime wall, timber hat, stone boots"

**Palette:**
- warm-white limewashed lime render;
- dark pigmented lime-cement plinth, with an optional hard tile or stone skirt to about +450 (the *namakokabe* idea, P11);
- natural-oiled larch soffit and fascia;
- warm-grey or dark-bronze PVDF metals (never copper, which stains light render green [S] ATAS-CU);
- oak bench.

**Key dimensions** [I unless tagged]:
- **Plinth:** as A (75 XPS + 12 parge, face x −88).
- **Wall build-up:** mineral wool 50; strapping 19 (cavity kept: the capillary break is mandatory in Vancouver [S] via 01); 12 mm cement board (06 A15c), or a wood-wool carrier.
- **Render:** three-coat lime with pozzolan on exposed elevations, about 3 + 15 + 10 = 28 mm [S] MW-REND. Face at x ≈ −122, so it overhangs the plinth by 34 mm.
- **Base:** render bottom at ≥ +250 on a stainless or PVC bellcast bead (no aluminium, 06). Flashing per §3.2(b) in the plinth colour.
- **Finish:** four coats of limewash, renewed every 3–7 yr; or silicate paint for a longer interval [S] MW-REND.
- **Eave:** unchanged, natural larch.

**Why it can age well:**
- Lime is vapour-open and repairable, and its colour variation is part of its appeal.
- The 600 mm eave protects the upper wall (P5, P9).
- Moss and algae on the lower wall can read as a living patina.

**Why it ages less well here:**
- Render on insulation greens: algae on north faces, dark moulds on south faces, worst on rough textures [S] JOH11.
- Vancouver's wet autumns sit in the growth peak (spring and autumn [S] JOH11).
- A white wall shows every streak.
- It needs limewash every 3–7 yr.
- **Lime render is a Part 5 cladding in Vancouver** (01/07).
- **Lime on wood-fibre without a cavity** (the UK/German system, BTE-PLINTH) would also fail the mandatory capillary break in a moisture index of 1.4 [S] via 01. Those systems also keep wood-fibre out of the splash zone, using XPS plinth insulation instead [S] BTE-PLINTH.

**Decades keyframes** [I]:

### 4.3 Choice for this pavilion, and what it implies

**I would choose A.** The reasons:
- The brief is a pavilion edge with a bench, with moss welcome and a "living edge".
- The living part belongs on the ground, plinth and bench, where moss harms nothing. The wall above should stay dry and crisp.
- A does exactly that, keeps Part 9, and needs the least frequent maintenance to look finished.
- B makes the whole wall the living surface, which reads beautifully in precedents (P9, P10, P11), but in Vancouver it means a Part 5 route, a limewash cycle of 3–7 years, and green streaks on a white wall.

**Implications of A:**

- **Base flashing.** It becomes a hidden, charcoal "drip in the shadow": nose 10 mm past the plinth face, 6 mm kick, no casing bead, no bare zinc or galvanized metal. Confirm with the reviewer that the code reading (drip measured from the face below) is acceptable (§5 C5).
- **Plinth height.** Keep the foundation top at +300 (06/07). The visible plinth is ≈ 217 mm plus a 23 mm shadow, with boards at +240. The plinth is low (about 1:13 of the wall height) and the volume reads as "floating", in the Vorarlberg manner (P6). If the architect wants a heavier base, or timber at ≥ +300 (the guide value for high rainfall [S] LUNA-NA/THERM-IG; DIN [S] BAUNETZ-ERD), the foundation top must rise to about +375. That puts a step or ramp at the door; it is the floor-level decision, not a finish decision.
- **Eave.** Light larch soffit with concealed vents. One dark band of fascia, edge metal and gutter. Optionally thin the fascia to about 320 mm (§3.6).
- **Gutter.** Keep it hung and removable, square, in the same charcoal. Optionally use rain chains into cobble basins. No concealed box gutter. Gutterless only on façades away from the bench.

## 6. Unverified and gaps

- **Precedent edges were not measured.** No photos or drawings were inspected (§0). Plinth heights, shadow-gap widths and gutter details of P1–P12 are unknown. Check them against the listed URLs, and against the detail.de drawings for P8.
- **Western red cedar charring in BC:** no source was found for local producers or for long-term Vancouver performance of charred WRC. P4 uses imported sugi.
- **Nakamoto figures** (80–150 yr, a 40 yr soot layer, the patina scale) are vendor observations from Japan. They are not independent field data.
- **Zinc patina timing, and PVDF colour-retention numbers** (ΔE over years): not sourced in this pass.
- **Concrete run-off staining:** only the general principle (BSD-013) and the colonisation evidence (02/04) were found. No architectural-concrete weathering guide was read.
- **Lime render in Vancouver:** no local field example was found. Algae timing is inferred from Swedish data (JOH11) and supplier guides.
- **"Shadow gap" dimensions** (20–30 mm minimum) are my judgement; no published source was found.
- **DIN 68800-2:** the 30 cm value is read via Baunetz Wissen. A reduced value with a gravel strip (often quoted as 15 cm) was seen only in a forum post and is **not** used.
- **Green roof edge** (VREF-7a4a, P2, P3, P12): the vegetation-free gravel margin usually required at roof edges (FLL guideline) was not read. If a living roof edge is pursued, read the FLL guideline and check the extra load in the physics file.
- **Search budget:** the shared web-search limit was reached during this task. Topics not reached include RDH/BC Housing guidance on stucco staining, an FPInnovations finishing guide, the full 2010 Wood Handbook chapter 16, and the Swiss Lignum façade guide.
