# 12 — Ground floor behind a flush, accessible glazed-wall threshold (Vancouver)

Researched: 2026-10-13. Status: **draft for the architect's review**. It is not an approved library entry.

**Brief.** The building is single-storey. A large sliding or folding glazed wall faces east or west and meets the ground at a near-flush, accessible threshold. Outside the threshold are permeable pavers. The architect proposed a concrete slab with a hempcrete topping and asked for a better option if there is one.

**Scope of this file.** This file covers the **interior ground floor** and its edge where it meets the door's bottom track. It also covers what the floor needs from the foundation. Two other researchers cover related elements:
- the door/threshold product, its drainage and the sill itself;
- the exterior paving.

This file only states what the floor needs from those two.

## 1. Bottom line

### 1.1 Recommendation

**Do not use hempcrete as the ground-floor layer.** Instead, build a **low-carbon concrete slab on grade on graphite EPS, with a sealed polyethylene soil-gas barrier and an exposed, ground/polished concrete finish (option c)**. Under the whole door track, add a **load-bearing cellular-glass thermal-break course** on an engineered **grade beam**.

The architect's concrete-plus-hempcrete floor loses on every target the brief sets except biogenic storage:

1. **Carbon.** A floor-grade hemp-lime mix needs about 200–275 mm (I) to reach RSI 2.45. It emits about 39–93 kg CO2e/m² fossil A1–A3 (I), because the lime binder content at floor density is high. That alone exceeds the whole concrete + HFO-XPS baseline (36–38 kg/m², I). It stores 35–65 kg CO2/m² biogenic (I), but net of storage it is still no better than the recommended slab.
2. **Moisture.** About 64–121 kg/m² (I) of mix water must dry **upward only**, through the wearing layer, because there is a slab and polyethylene below. In Vancouver that delays finishes by months [I]. It also leaves a hygroscopic layer on the coldest surface in the room, at the glazed edge [I].
3. **Wear and track support.** At 90 days the floor mix reaches only 0.45–1.50 MPa (S, GOL25), so it needs a separate stiff wearing screed. It cannot provide the rigid seat a door track needs [I]; the reference sill must be level within ±1 mm (S, MAR).
4. **Code.** Hempcrete is not a VBBL-listed insulation (V2 9.25.2.2). Part 10 describes slab insulation as "under all slab area" (V1 Table 10.2.2.6). Both points need an alternative solution and an energy-staff ruling [S].

The recommended floor:
- meets the code prescriptively (Part 9 + Part 10);
- puts the thermal mass where east or west sun lands;
- gives a hard, repairable mineral surface at the heavily used threshold;
- keeps the door load off the floor, so the slab stays a non-structural Part 9 floor-on-ground (V2 9.16.1.1–9.16.1.2) [S].

### 1.4 What the threshold and the foundation must provide

**Threshold (door product and threshold researcher):**
- A sill exposed **≤ 13 mm**, bevelled, above FF (S, V1 3.8.3.6.(5)). The reference sill sits 5 mm proud (S, MAR). With the pavers at FF −10 [08b], the total rise is 15 mm (I); see §8 item 8.
- A recessed-sill slot **67 mm** deep from FF (S, MAR). It is **sill depth + 51 mm** front to back: 224–249 mm for 2 tracks, 321–346 mm for 3 tracks (S). Its length is **system width + 51 mm** (S).
- The slot is level within 6 mm and the sill within 1 mm along its whole length (S).
- After the sill is set, the slot is filled with chloride-free self-levelling grout (S).
- No moisture-sensitive flooring (wood) within **610 mm** of the interior face of the sill (S, MAR).
- An exterior channel drain that carries sill drainage and surface water to storm, **not** into the paver base [I].
- Door U ≤ 1.44 (performance path) / 1.22 (prescriptive) W/m²K (S, V1 Table 10.2.2.7.(1), "sliding and folding doors with glazing").

**Foundation:**
- An engineered (Part 4) **reinforced grade beam on a strip footing** along the whole glazed wall.
- Footing underside **≥ 450 mm below adjacent finished grade** (S, V1 1.1.3.2), or **1.2 m on clay** unless engineered (S, [07 E2]).
- Beam top at about **FF − 167 mm** (I), carrying a **100 mm FOAMGLAS PERINSUL HL course** (λD 0.058 W/mK, CS 2.75 MPa (S)) on which the sill slot sits.
- On the beam's exterior face: dampproofing (required, because exterior grade is above the interior ground level), 75–100 mm XPS, a footing capillary break, and a perimeter drain.
- The **paver base within 5 m of the building must not infiltrate** (S, [07 E10]: VBBL infiltration setback). Line it and slope it away to an under-drain.

**Derived values [I]:**
- **Thickness for RSI 2.45** = (2.45 − 0.16 film − 0.04 slab) × λ = **198 mm** (lightest F mix) to **274 mm** (densest F mix). A screed adds little resistance and is ignored.
- **Water to dry out** = fresh density − 50 % RH density, multiplied by thickness:
  - light mix: (690 − 366) × 0.198 = **64 kg/m²**;
  - dense mix: (930 − 487) × 0.274 = **121 kg/m²**.

  For comparison, a 100 mm concrete slab holds roughly 10 kg/m² of evaporable water [I, assumption: about half of 195 kg/m³ mix water at w/cm 0.65].
- **Heat-exchange ability (effusivity √(λρc)):** hempcrete about 170–230 W·s½/m²K vs concrete about 2,250–2,450. Inputs: λ, ρ and c from GOL25; concrete λ 2.5 from the VBBL table factor of 0.0004 RSI per mm (S); concrete ρ 2,300–2,400 and c 880–1,000 J/kgK (I, typical values). Hempcrete therefore feels warm underfoot but cannot absorb sun into its mass quickly [I].

### 3.5 The door's bottom track (reference product)

These values are all (S) from MAR.

**Recessed or flush sill, general:**

- The slot is level within **6 mm** across the opening.

**Recessed-sill slot geometry:**
- Overall recess **67 mm** from FF, including **25 mm** of shim space below the sill.

**Other requirements:**
- Fill the slot after levelling with self-levelling concrete **free of chlorinated additives**.

### 4.1 (a) Architect's: structural concrete slab + hempcrete topping + wearing layer

| # | layer (top down) | thickness | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| a1 | Lime screed (wearing / load-spreading layer), or timber on battens | 50–75 (I) | Needed: the hempcrete is 0.45–1.50 MPa (S). Underfloor heating pipes go here, in ≥ 70 mm (S, LCO) |
| a2 | Hempcrete, floor mix | **200–275 (I)** for RSI 2.45; 300 (I) for a moisture margin | Cast and tamped wet, on the slab |
| a3 | Concrete slab | 100 | ≥ 20 MPa (S) |
| a4 | Polyethylene 0.25 mm, sealed | — | Under the slab (V2 9.25.3.6.(2)(a)) |
| a5 | Clear stone + radon rough-in | ≥ 100 | (S) |

**Thermal:**
- 2.45 is reached only with ≥ 200–275 mm of hempcrete (I).
- The slab beneath is **uninsulated and cold**, close to ground temperature [I].
- At the door the hempcrete must stop: the track needs a rigid seat. The concrete strip under the track then links the warm floor directly to the exterior paving unless a separate break is added [I].
- The VBBL wording "insulation **under** all slab area" makes a topping-only design an interpretation question [S/I].

**Moisture:**
- 64–121 kg/m² of water dries upward only, through the screed and finish (I). Expect several months before vapour-sensitive finishes go down [I].
- The LCO rule for timber on limecrete is ≥ 3 months (S); a thicker, wetter hempcrete layer will take longer [I].
- In service, interior vapour diffuses down through the vapour-open hempcrete (μ 6–10 (S)) to the cold slab top. The worst case is the glazed edge, where the slab is coldest [I].
- No measured data on hempcrete-on-slab interface RH in a marine climate were found [gap].

**Soil gas:** The polyethylene sits under the slab, as required [S]. The hempcrete is irrelevant to the barrier, but it must not be holed by fixings for the screed or track [I].

**Capillary break:** The stone and polyethylene below the slab [S]. **Drying path:** upward only [I].

**Embodied carbon (A1–A3 fossil, I):**

| layer | kg CO2e/m² |
|---|---|
| slab | 15.2–19.7 |
| stone | 0.2–1.8 |
| hempcrete (198–274 mm) | 38.5–92.7 |
| 50 mm lime screed (pro-rated from SA-FDES) | 9.2 |
| **total** | **63–124** |

- **Biogenic stored −35 to −65 kg CO2/m² (I).**
- Possible long-term carbonation uptake 14–37 kg/m² (I), only if the layer carbonates through its depth under a finish. This is doubtful given the 6 cm-in-240-days observation (S, ARE).

**Structure and wear:**
- 0.45–1.50 MPa at 90 days (S).
- Point loads, wheelchairs and furniture need the screed [I]. Screed cracking over a compliant base is likely [I].
- It cannot form or carry the track recess, so a concrete strip ≥ 610 mm wide is needed at the door anyway (MAR rule for moisture-sensitive floors) [I].

**Code path:**
- Concrete slab: Part 9 ✓.
- Hempcrete as insulation: not listed (9.25.2.2), so an **alternative solution** is needed.
- Part 10 "under slab" wording: needs a ruling from City energy staff.
- No North American code text covers hemp-lime floors (Appendix BL covers walls (S, WA-BL)).

**Cost tier:** $$$ (I). Two wet trades plus a long drying hold.

**BC availability:** Poor (I). There is no confirmed Canadian Tradical distributor (S\*). Hemp hurd comes from the Prairies (S\*). There are few experienced installers.

**Ageing [I]:**

| horizon | expected state |
|---|---|
| 0–1 yr | Long drying; delayed finish. |
| 5 yr | Hairline screed cracks along furniture and wheel lanes; the threshold strip wears differently from the hempcrete field. |
| 15 yr | Screed renewal at the traffic lane. Hidden damp at the slab interface near the glazing if the door leaks or condensation recurs. |
| 30 yr | Re-screed. The hempcrete itself is stable if kept dry; if wetted repeatedly, the binder–shiv bond weakens (no data). |

**Variant (a′):** keep the code insulation (EPS) under the slab and add 50–75 mm of hempcrete on top for "feel" and buffering. This adds about 10–25 kg/m² fossil and −9 to −18 biogenic (I), and keeps the drying and wear problems. **Not recommended.**

**Radon:**
- The UK practice has **no membrane** (S, LCO; SA-LC).

**Capillary break and drying:** Excellent capillary break (S, IBP, LCO). In (b) the drying path runs downward into the drained FGA as well as upward [I]. In (b′) it is upward only.

**Moisture risk in Vancouver:** FGA wetted to about 8 vol % loses about 45 % of its insulating value (λ 0.090 → 0.165 (S, IBP, garbled table)). On perched-water tills in the October–March wet season ([07 E.2(c)]), the FGA must stay **above** any water table and be drained [I].

**Code path:**

### 4.3 (c) Low-carbon concrete slab on graphite EPS — RECOMMENDED

The full layer table is in §6. In short: 100 mm 25 MPa GUL concrete with 25–40 % SCM, 0.25 mm polyethylene, 100 mm graphite EPS, ≥ 100 mm clear stone, and the radon rough-in.

**Thermal:**
- RSI **3.00 (I)** with the code-table EPS value, or 3.33–3.43 (I) with the graphite manufacturer value. This is ≥ 2.45 ✓.
- Edge: the EPS meets a PERINSUL course under the door and the exterior-insulated stem elsewhere ([09 P1]).

**Moisture:** Stone capillary break (S). Polyethylene under the slab; the slab dries upward. With an exposed or polished finish there is no moisture-sensitive layer to trap [I]. The slab is 100 mm with about 10 kg/m² of evaporable water (I).

**Soil gas:** Prescriptive. Polyethylene over EPS in intimate contact with the slab (S, Note A-9.25.3.6), sealed to every perimeter element (§6.3).

**Embodied carbon (A1–A3 fossil, I):**

| layer | kg CO2e/m² |
|---|---|
| 25 MPa slab, GUL + 40 % SCM | 17.1 |
| 25 MPa slab, GUL + 25 % SCM (alternative) | 19.7 |
| graphite EPS | 4.1–4.7 |
| stone | 0.2–1.8 |
| **total** | **21.4–26.2** (21–24 with the 40 % SCM mix) |

- With a 56-day low-carbon mix (Lafarge 111 kg/m³ (S\*, [09])) the total is about 15.4.
- Biogenic: 0.

**Structure and wear:**
- 25 MPa is chosen over the code's 20 MPa (S) for a ground or polished wearing surface at a heavily used threshold [I].
- The slab is non-structural (the door load goes to the beam) [S, 9.16.1].

**Comfort:**
- High mass, about 200–240 kJ/m²K (I), and high effusivity, so the floor absorbs east or west sun patches [I] (§6.5(d)).
- It feels cooler to bare feet than timber or cork [I; S, OLE: comfort for bare feet depends on floor material].

**Code path:** Part 9 + Part 10 prescriptive. Part 4 applies to the grade beam (structure is Part 4 for all but ≤ 2-unit dwellings [S, 09 / V1 1.3.3.2(3)]). A 56-day mix needs the engineer's acceptance [S, 09].

**Cost tier:** $$ (I). **BC availability:** all local. The concrete is made by Concrete BC EPD producers (S); graphite EPS is available in BC (S\*, [09]). PERINSUL is an Owens Corning FOAMGLAS product. The North American variant ("PERINSUL SIB", R-2.4/in) was noted only in a search summary in 09 [S\*]. Confirm stock.

### 4.4 (c2) The same slab on FGA instead of EPS + stone

- About 250–315 mm of compacted FGA under geotextile and 0.25 mm polyethylene, then the 100 mm slab.
- Capillary break, gas layer and insulation are combined. The materials are inert and non-leaching (S, GLV), and FGA is reusable as "clean fill" (S, GLV TDS).
- Carbon: 17–27 kg/m² (I) **before** freight, plus about 24–36 for freight (I).
- Code path: alternative solution for the FGA insulation (not listed) [S].
- Cost tier: $$$ (I). BC availability: none (S\*).
- **Becomes the preferred variant if a Pacific Northwest FGA source appears** [I].

### 4.5 (d) Suspended timber floor on screw piles with a ventilated underfloor

- Taken from [09 G2]: 38 × 235 joists with cellulose, about RSI 4.5 (I) against the 4.22 required (S).
- About 16–20 kg/m² fossil, about 50 kg/m² stored (I, [09]).
- No soil-gas rough-in, because the floor is not in contact with the ground [S, 9.13.4.1].

**Fails the brief at the threshold:**
- A suspended floor needs a ventilated clearance below. [09] used ≥ 450 mm (I), so FF sits at about +450–600 [09].

**Other risks:**
- The underfloor air is humid (RH about 86 % at 06:00 (S, [03])).

**Low thermal mass:** sun patches heat the timber surface quickly [I].

**Code path:** Part 4 piles; 3.8 accessible route. **Cost tier:** $$$ (I).

### 6.3 Sealing sequence

**(i) Soil-gas barrier to the foundation**
- At stem walls, the polyethylene is turned up the slab edge and sealed to the concrete before the pour (S, CGSB 7.1.4.5.5 via [07]).
- After curing, the slab perimeter joint gets flexible sealant (S).
- At the door, the polyethylene is turned up the PERINSUL interior face and lapped onto the pan membrane (I).

**(ii) Floor barrier to the door's air seal**
- The pan membrane receives the sill's interior air-seal bead (I).
- From there the door's gaskets and frame carry the air barrier up to the wall air barrier at the jambs. This is the threshold researcher's scope.
- The pan's end dams turn up the jambs and lap under the jamb air-barrier membrane (I).

**Sequence (I):**
1. Beam.
2. PERINSUL course.
3. Pan membrane.
4. Stone, EPS and polyethylene, with the upturn lapped to the pan.
5. XPS edge strip.
6. Slab pour.
7. Door sill on shims, then grout.
8. Sill-to-slab sealant.
9. Radon test after occupancy.

### 6.4 What the floor needs from the foundation

1. **A grade beam under the full glazed opening** (Part 4).
   - It carries the door's line load and limits differential settlement, because the sill needs ±1 mm (S) and the header ≤ 3 mm deflection (S, MAR).
   - Top at FF −167 (I) for a 100 mm course; FF −132 (I) for a 65 mm course.
   - Width ≥ the course width (about 300–365 mm (I)).
2. **Footing depth ≥ 450 mm below adjacent finished grade** (S). Use 1.2 m on clay unless engineered (S, Table 9.12.2.2 via [07 E2]). The slab is fully insulated, so the frost depth applies (S, A-4.2.4.4.(1); 9.12.2.2.(2) via [07]).
3. **Dampproofing, drainage and capillary break.** Interior FF ≈ exterior grade at the door, and the interior ground level (top of stone) is about 300 mm below it. So the beam must have:
   - exterior dampproofing (S rule);
   - a perimeter drain with its top below the slab underside (S, [07 E8]);
   - a footing capillary break ([07 E4]).
   - Do **not** connect the sub-slab stone to the perimeter drain: an open drain would short-circuit future depressurisation [I].
4. **No infiltration next to the building.** The VBBL note requires infiltration systems ≥ 5 m from buildings (S, [07 E10]). The permeable-paver base next to the door must be **lined, sloped away and under-drained**. Otherwise rain is fed to the beam, the sub-slab stone and the footing [I].
   - [08b M] already does this: HDPE liner turned up against the foundation/slab edge, lined landing from 0 to +1850 mm, 2 % fall to an underdrain (I values in 08b).
   - The liner upturn must lap **under** the beam's exterior XPS and dampproofing, never between the PERINSUL course and the pan membrane [I].
5. **Grading elsewhere.** With FF at grade on the glazed side, the other walls still need foundation exposure ≥ 150 mm (S, 9.15.4.6) and cladding ≥ 200 mm above ground (S, 9.27.2.4). The ground must fall to ≤ FF −200 around the rest of the building, or the terrace must be a drained plinth [I]. This is a site-planning requirement.
6. **Alternatives:**
   - (e1) **insulated raft / FPSF**: thickened edge carries the track; XPS or cellular-glass wings under the lined paver base; Part 4 + geotechnical.
   - **helical piles + grade beam** on poor soils: Part 4 ([09 P3]).
   - In both, the PERINSUL course and pan membrane logic are unchanged [I].

#### (b) Thermal

- Calculated RSI is 3.00–3.43 (I), above 2.45 (S). Edge losses at the door are not included (ψ unknown).
- The door edge is the coldest line in the room. MAR warns of interior frost at sills in cold climates (S). The Vancouver January 2.5 % design temperature is −7 °C (S, [07]).
- The PERINSUL course and the 25 mm XPS strip raise the interior surface temperature at the slab edge, but by an unquantified amount [I].
- An optional low-temperature hydronic loop within 600 mm of the door is the fallback the door maker suggests (S). The floor above it still needs 2.45 to the soil (S, 10.2.2.6.(1)(g)).

#### (c) Moisture

- No hygroscopic layer, so nothing can be trapped [I].
- Construction moisture: about 10 kg/m² (I), drying upward over weeks to months. Test the slab before any timber or cork goes on beyond the apron (I; S for the MC principle, LCO).
- The ground side is closed by the polyethylene [S].
- In Vancouver the slab sits on stone drained by the perimeter drain [I].

#### (d) Comfort with east/west sun

- Low east and west sun reaches deep into the room through large glazing [I].
- The exposed 100 mm slab, insulated below, stores about 200–240 kJ/m²K (I) and has high effusivity. It absorbs the morning (east) or afternoon (west) sun patch and lowers peak surface temperatures compared with a low-effusivity timber, cork or hempcrete surface [I].
- **Limits:**
  - ASHRAE 55: floor surface 19–29 °C in the occupied zone (S, ASH55 5.3.4.4).
  - EN 1264 allows 35 °C within 1 m of exterior windows (S, OLE).
- **West glazing:** peak sun coincides with peak air temperature, so overheating risk is higher [I]. The mass helps only if it is night-flushed and the glazing is shaded. Exterior shading for east and west sun is an envelope decision outside this file [I].
- Use a mid-to-light ground-concrete colour to cut peak sun-patch temperatures [I].
- **Moisture buffering:** a densified concrete floor buffers little [I]. Put buffering surfaces (clay or lime plaster, hemp-lime) on walls or ceilings, where hemp's buffering (MBV 2.34 rendered (S, [02])) works without traffic or ground moisture [I].

## 7. Conflicts between sources (flagged)

1. **Slab RSI wording vs a topping design.** VBBL Table 10.2.2.6 says "insulation **under** all slab area and around edge of slab" (S). The architect's hempcrete is **on top**. Isothermal-plane summing (Note A-10.2.2.6) would count it, but whether the prescriptive row accepts a topping is a City interpretation. Open.
2. **Breathable-floor practice vs VBBL.** UK limecrete and hempcrete floors use no DPM (S, LCO; SA-LC: "damp-proof membranes are not necessary"). VBBL requires an air barrier of CGSB-51.34 polyethylene under every floor on ground (S, 9.13.4.2.(1), 9.25.3.6.(1)).
3. **Membrane thickness.** VBBL Note: 0.15 mm (S). CGSB-149.11-2019: 0.25 mm (S, [07 D.3]). This file specifies 0.25 mm.
4. **FGA conductivity.** Glavel: R-1.7/in, λ ≈ 0.085 (S, dry, manufacturer). German approvals: design λ 0.11–0.14 (S, IBP); field moisture can raise λ to about 0.165 (S, garbled table). This file uses the design values, which makes the FGA about 30–65 % thicker than Glavel's figure implies (I).
5. **FGA as the radon layer.** Glavel says it supports passive radon systems and that active systems may reduce thermal performance (S). IBP found convection losses under slabs even without a fan (S). VBBL allows any material that allows effective depressurisation (S). The thermal penalty is unquantified.
6. **Hempcrete "carbon negative" claims vs the floor mix.**
   - Lime Technology: "carbon capture 130 kg CO2/m³" at 275 kg/m³ (S, LCO factsheet).
   - Šinka et al.: −46.5 to −68.6 kg CO2/m³ for lime-based composites (S\*, search summary).
   - The floor mix calculated here: fossil 195–339, biogenic −175 to −236, **net +20 to +103 kg CO2e/m³** before carbonation (I).
   - The difference comes from binder dosage (floor binder : hemp is 2.25 vs about 1–1.8 for walls/roofs (S, GOL25)) and the lime factor (0.94 (S\*) vs 1.2 (S)).
   - Carbonation could recover 0.325–0.462 kg CO2 per kg of binder (S, ARE), but under a screed it may take decades or never happen (Arrigoni: none below 6 cm after 240 days (S, ARE)).
7. **Hemp-lime strength.** LCO cubes: 1 N/mm² at 35 days and 2.4 N/mm² at 215 days (S). GOL25 F-series at 90 days: 0.45–1.50 MPa (S). Mixes and ages differ.
8. **Limecrete vs the Part 9 concrete minimum.** 3–4 MPa at 28 days (S, SA-FDES) vs ≥ 20 MPa for floors (S, 9.3.1.6). Limecrete is not "concrete" under 9.16.4, so it needs an alternative solution.
9. **Wood flooring at the door.** MAR forbids moisture-sensitive flooring within 610 mm of the sill (S). The brief suggested a timber or cork finish. Resolved here with a mineral apron.
10. **Plinth height logic.** 07 D placed the slab and stem top at +200, and 09 at +300, above grade (S). At the flush glazed wall, FF ≈ grade. The 9.15.4.6 / 9.27.2.4 clearances still apply at the other walls, so the site must fall away there (§6.4.5). This is a design change, not a contradiction.
11. **Permeable pavers vs the infiltration setback.** Pavers at the door vs the VBBL 5 m infiltration setback (S, [07 E10]). Resolved by lining the base near the building. Passed to the paving researcher.
12. **Graphite EPS RSI.** 09 used λ 0.031–0.032 (RSI 3.1–3.2 per 100 mm (S\*/I)). The VBBL/BCBC table value for EPS Type 2 gives 2.80 (S). Both pass; this file reports the conservative 3.00.
13. **Foundation above finished ground at the door.** VBBL 9.15.4.6.(1) requires exterior foundation walls ≥ 150 mm above finished ground, with no exception in the text (S, flagged in [08b]). At the flush threshold the grade-beam top is at about FF −167 and the pavers at about FF −10 (I), so the beam is wholly below grade.
    - Readings: the door opening has no wall or cladding, and the PERINSUL course plus pan membrane does the protective job, **or** an alternative solution is needed.
    - Shared open question with [08b] (§8).
14. **09 G1 vs this file.** 09 recommended a 56-day mix (S\*) for the lowest carbon. For a polished wearing floor at a heavily used threshold, this file prefers a 28-day 25 MPa GUL + 25–40 % SCM mix (I). The 56-day mix stays as an option if the polishing schedule allows (engineer).

## 8. Open questions

1. **Occupancy** (as in [09]). Part 10 house table vs ASHRAE 90.1/NECB, and whether 3.8 accessibility applies to the whole building.
2. **City energy staff:** does a hempcrete or other topping count toward "insulation under all slab area"? Does the slab-edge strip at the door need the full 2.45?
3. **Door product:** type (lift-slide, multi-slide, bifold), number of tracks, panel weights and line load, recess dimensions, drained or undrained sill, and its tested USI. These fix the slot (§6.2) and the beam design.
4. **THERM model** of the door edge (slab, EPS, PERINSUL, beam, sill) for the ψ-value and the interior surface temperature at −7 °C (S design). This decides whether a perimeter heating loop is needed.
5. **Soils and groundwater:** clay (1.2 m footing), till with perched water, or sand. Hydrostatic conditions exclude (a), (b) and (d) (S, 9.16.3.2).
6. **PERINSUL availability in Canada** (Owens Corning FOAMGLAS North America; "PERINSUL SIB" (S\*)) and its acceptance as a structural thermal break under Part 4. Alternatives: a Compacfoam- or Purenit-type block (S\*), checked against wet exposure.
7. **FGA supply west of the Rockies.** If one appears, (c2) overtakes (c) on moisture, health and end of life. Re-check freight.
8. **Threshold datum and rise.** [08b] sets the paver and grate top at FF −10. With the reference sill 5 mm proud, the rise is 15 mm, over the 13 mm limit (S). Options: a sill with a ≤ 8 mm exposed lip, a bevelled transition, or a paver top at FF −8 (I). This is for the door researcher ([11]).
9. **VBBL 9.15.4.6 at the door opening** (§7, conflict 13): City interpretation or alternative solution.
10. **Hemp-lime floor data not read:** the French *Règles professionnelles* (floor application), BRE *Hemp Lime Construction*, Arnaud & Gourlay, IsoHemp, and the full IRC Appendix BL. They could change the strength and drying figures in §3.1, but not the carbon or code findings [I].
11. **No field data** on hempcrete-on-slab interface humidity, or on hemp-lime floor drying times, in a marine climate.
